Privacy policy

Mach, the mail app for Gmail and Outlook on Mac and iPhone · Ahmed Khaleel · Effective 12 October 2026

Mach runs on your Mac or iPhone and talks to Google or Microsoft directly from there. Your mail, your contacts' names, addresses and pictures, and your Google or Microsoft sign-in are kept on your device. The developer runs no server that receives any of it, and cannot see it. One small server exists, for one thing: telling an iPhone that an Outlook account has new mail. It is told no address, no sign-in and no mail (what it holds).

01Who is responsible

Mach is made and published by Ahmed Khaleel, an individual developer based in Ontario, Canada. Questions about this policy go to ahmed@gitdiagram.com.

This policy covers the Mach apps for Mac and iPhone as the developer publishes them (the downloads on GitHub, and the TestFlight and App Store builds when they exist) and this website. Mach's source is open; a copy that someone else has changed and built is theirs to answer for.

02Google data Mach uses, and why

When you sign in with Google, Mach asks for four permissions. These are all of them, and each is used only for the feature beside it.

Read, compose and send your Gmailgmail.modify
What Mach reads: your Gmail address, your messages and conversations with their attachments, labels and drafts, your send-as names and signatures, and Gmail's record of what changed since the last check.
What Mach changes, when you do it: it sends mail and saves drafts, and it archives, trashes, stars, marks read or unread and snoozes conversations by changing their labels (snoozing adds a hidden label of Mach's own to your Gmail).
Why: this is the mail app itself. This permission does not let an app delete mail for good past the Trash.
See your contactscontacts.readonly
What Mach reads: three things about each contact: their name, their email addresses and the address of their picture. No phone numbers, postal addresses or notes.
Why: to show a sender's face beside their mail, in lists, conversations and notifications, and to offer the people you know, by name, when you address a message.
See your "Other contacts"contacts.other.readonly
What Mach reads: the same three things, for people you have written with whom Google saved automatically.
Why: the same pictures and the same suggestions. Most of the people who write to you are here, not in your contacts.
See your basic profileuserinfo.profile
What Mach reads: your own profile picture, and the email addresses on your profile that it belongs to.
Why: to show it beside your own messages.

Mach never changes your contacts or your Google profile. You can turn pictures off in Mach's settings ("Profile pictures"); the mail app works the same without them. With pictures off, Mach still reads your contacts' names and addresses, about once a day, to offer them when you address a message.

03Microsoft data Mach uses, and why

When you sign in with Microsoft (an Outlook, Hotmail or Live account), Mach asks for six permissions. These are all of them, and each is used only for the feature beside it.

Read and write access to your mailMail.ReadWrite
What Mach reads: your messages and conversations with their attachments, folders, categories and drafts, and Outlook's record of what changed since the last check.
What Mach changes, when you do it: it saves drafts, moves conversations between folders (archive, trash, spam), flags them, marks them read or unread, and snoozes them (snoozing adds a category of Mach's own to the message).
Why: this is the mail app itself.
Send mail as youMail.Send
What Mach does: it sends the mail you write, when you send it.
Why: sending is a separate permission at Microsoft.
Read your profileUser.Read
What Mach reads: your name and your address.
Why: to know which account you signed in to.
Read your contactsContacts.Read
What Mach reads: the names and email addresses of the contacts saved in your account, and the picture saved on a contact when mail from that person is shown.
Why: to offer them when you address a message, and to show their picture beside their mail. Kept on your device.
Read your relevant people listPeople.Read
What Mach reads: the names and email addresses of the people you write with most, as Outlook lists them.
Why: to offer them when you address a message. Kept on your device.
Maintain access to data you have given it access tooffline_access
What it does: it lets Mach stay signed in, so you are not asked to sign in again each time. It gives access to nothing more.

Mach never changes your contacts, and it does not read your calendar. It reads the two lists about once a day. An account signed in before 12 October 2026 was not asked for the two contact permissions and Mach reads no contacts for it until you sign it in again; everything else works the same without them. A work or school account may not be able to sign in at all: Microsoft lets an organization refuse apps whose publisher it has not verified, and Mach's is not. Mach uses Microsoft data only to provide the mail features you see in the app. It does not use it for advertising or to train AI or machine-learning models, it does not transfer it to anyone, and no human reads it.

04Where it is kept, and for how long

On your device, and nowhere else.

Your mail
A database in the app's own data folder (on a Mac, ~/Library/Application Support/Mach). It holds what Mach has fetched so that reading and search are instant and work offline.
Your Google sign-in
The tokens Google issues are kept in the system keychain of the device, one entry per account. Mach does not mark them for iCloud Keychain syncing. Mach never sees or stores your Google password: you type it on Google's own page.
Your Microsoft sign-in
The same. The tokens Microsoft issues are kept in the system keychain of the device, and Mach never sees or stores your Microsoft password: you type it on Microsoft's own page.
Contacts' names and addresses
In the same database as your mail, per account, refreshed about once a day. They are what Mach offers when you address a message.
Contact pictures
The list of email address to picture address, per account, in the same data folder, refreshed about once a day. The pictures themselves, and company logos, in the app's cache folder.
Attachments
The ones Mach has shown you a preview of, or that you opened, are downloaded once and kept in the app's cache folder.
Settings
Your choices in the app (theme, text size, swipes and so on) in the app's preferences.

It stays until you sign out of the account or delete the app; see Removing your data. Mach adds no time limit of its own, because it is your own copy of your own mailbox.

If you back up your device (Time Machine, iCloud Backup, a backup to a computer), your backup may include Mach's data like any other app's. That is between you and Apple; Mach does not send it anywhere.

05What leaves your device, and to whom

The developer receives nothing. These are all the places the app connects to.

Google
Sign-in, Gmail and contacts, over HTTPS, directly from your device, under your own account. Everything you do in Mach (sending, archiving, searching the whole mailbox) is a request to Gmail. Contact pictures are downloaded from the addresses Google gives.
Microsoft
Sign-in and Outlook mail, through Microsoft Graph, over HTTPS, directly from your device, under your own account. In an Outlook account, everything you do in Mach is a request to Microsoft.
Company logos
When a sender has no Google picture, Mach looks for the company's certified logo the way Gmail does (the BIMI standard). It asks Google's public DNS service for a record on the sender's domain, and downloads the logo certificate from wherever that record points, usually the company's or its certificate authority's server. These requests carry no sign-in and no part of your mail, but they do show the sender's domain to Google's DNS service and your IP address to the certificate's host. Turning off "Profile pictures" stops them.
Senders of your mail
Pictures, fonts and styling inside an email load from the sender's servers when you open it, as in most mail apps. That can tell a sender that you opened their message, roughly when, and your IP address. Mach has no switch to stop this yet. The system web view that draws the email keeps its own cache of what it loaded, and any cookies those servers set, on your device. Scripts in mail never run, and forms in mail cannot send.
Links you click
Open in your browser, outside Mach.
GitHub (Mac only)
The Mac app checks GitHub about once an hour for a new version and downloads it from there. It is a plain file download; GitHub sees your IP address as it would for any download. Nothing about you or your mail is sent.
Apple
On iPhone you get the app and its updates through Apple. A new-mail banner is written on the phone itself. For a Gmail account nothing passes through Apple's notification service at all. For an Outlook account a notification does, carrying one thing: the id Outlook gave the new message, which means nothing without your sign-in. The sender, subject and first words are fetched from Outlook by the phone and never pass through Apple or the developer.

06What Mach never does

  • No server of the developer's receives, stores or processes your mail, your contacts, your address or your sign-in. The one server there is, for Outlook notifications on iPhone, is told none of them (section 08).
  • No analytics, no usage tracking, no crash reporting service, no advertising, no third-party SDKs that collect data.
  • Your data is not sold, rented, shared or transferred to anyone.
  • Your Google data is not used to develop, improve or train AI or machine-learning models. Mach has no AI features.
  • Your Microsoft data is not used for advertising, or to develop, improve or train AI or machine-learning models.
  • No person reads your mail. The developer has no access to it.
  • There is no Mach account. You only ever sign in to Google or Microsoft.

07Google API Services: Limited Use

Mach's use and transfer to any other app of information received from Google APIs will adhere to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

In particular, and as described above:

  • Mach uses Google user data only to provide the mail features you see in the app.
  • Mach does not transfer Google user data to anyone. It stays on your device.
  • Mach does not use Google user data for advertising of any kind.
  • No human reads Google user data. The developer cannot reach it.
  • Mach does not use Google user data to train generalized or any other AI or machine-learning models.

The use of information received from Google Workspace APIs will likewise adhere to the Google Workspace API user data and developer policy, including its Limited Use requirements.

08Notifications for Outlook on iPhone: the one server, and what it holds

An iPhone can only be told about new mail through Apple's notification service, and something has to be awake to tell Apple. For Outlook accounts the developer runs one small server for that. It is built to know as little as it can.

What your iPhone sends it
For each Outlook account: a random id and a random secret that the phone made up, and the phone's notification address (the token Apple gives an app so it can be sent notifications). Not your email address, your name, your Microsoft sign-in or anything from your mailbox.
What Outlook sends it
Your phone asks Outlook, with the sign-in that stays on the phone, to call the server when unread mail reaches your inbox. Each call carries the secret and the id Outlook gave the new message: a long string that says nothing about the mail and opens nothing without your sign-in. No sender, subject or text.
What it does
It checks the secret and sends your phone a notification carrying that message id. Your phone then asks Outlook itself for the sender, subject and first words, and writes the banner. The message id is passed on and not kept.
What it keeps
One record per Outlook account per phone: a fingerprint of the id, a fingerprint of the secret, and the notification address, encrypted. There is no list of people, and no record says whose it is.
For how long
Until you sign that account out in Mach, which deletes the record at once and tells Outlook to stop calling. If you delete the app instead, the record is deleted when Apple reports the phone gone, or by itself 15 days after Mach last ran, whichever is first; Outlook stops calling within a week.
Who can read it
Nobody else's phone: a record can only be changed or deleted by someone who has its secret, and there is no key shared between people. It runs on Cloudflare, which hosts it and, like any host, sees the connections made to it.

Gmail accounts do not use this server or any other. Nothing of a Google account is sent to it. Mach finds new Gmail when it is open and when iOS lets it check in the background, and makes those banners on the phone, so they can arrive later than Outlook's.

The Mac app uses no server for either service. Mach's source also includes a second, fuller relay that a person can run for themselves on their own Cloudflare account; it is not part of the published apps and the developer does not run one for the public. If you run one, your device sends your relay your address, your sign-in token and your iPhone's notification address, and it reads the sender, subject and first words of new mail. All of that is on a server you own; the developer has no access to it.

09Security

  • Mach's own connections, to Google, to Microsoft and to GitHub, use HTTPS. A picture inside an old email can still load over plain HTTP if its sender wrote it that way.
  • Sign-in happens on Google's own page, in your browser on a Mac and in the system sign-in sheet on iPhone, using PKCE. Mach never handles your password.
  • Microsoft sign-in works the same way: on Microsoft's own page, in your browser on a Mac and in the system sign-in sheet on iPhone, using PKCE. Mach never sees your password, and its Microsoft sign-in has no secret key.
  • Sign-in tokens are in the system keychain. The mail database is protected by the device itself: your passcode and Apple's file encryption on iPhone, your login and FileVault (if you have it on) on a Mac. Mach adds no encryption of its own on top.
  • An open email is shown under a policy that runs no scripts from mail.
  • The source is public, so every claim here can be checked: github.com/ahmedkhaleel2004/mach.

If you find a security problem, please email ahmed@gitdiagram.com before making it public.

10Removing your data and Mach's access

  • Sign out. In Mach's settings, "Sign out" beside an account deletes that account's mail from Mach's database and its sign-in from the keychain. For an Outlook account on iPhone it also deletes the record the notification server held and tells Outlook to stop calling it.
  • Take away Mach's access at Google. Open myaccount.google.com/permissions, choose Mach and remove its access. From then on the sign-in on any device is worthless.
  • Take away Mach's access at Microsoft. For a personal account, open account.microsoft.com/privacy/app-access; for a work or school account, open myapps.microsoft.com. Choose Mach and remove its access.
  • Delete the app. On iPhone, deleting Mach removes everything it stored. On a Mac, sign out first, move Mach to the Trash, and to clear what is left delete ~/Library/Application Support/Mach, the avatars4, attachments and com.ahmedkhaleel.mach.mac folders in ~/Library/Caches, and the items named com.ahmedkhaleel.mach.mac in ~/Library/WebKit, ~/Library/HTTPStorages and ~/Library/Preferences.

None of this touches your mail at Google: it is all still in Gmail. The same goes for Outlook mail at Microsoft. Because the developer holds none of your data, there is nothing for him to look up, correct or delete on request, but you are welcome to ask. If you are in Canada and are unhappy with an answer, you can also contact the Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada.

11Children

Mach is not directed to children under 13, and the developer does not knowingly collect personal information from anyone, of any age. To use Mach you need a Google or Microsoft account, and each has its own age requirements.

12This website

mach.ahmedkhaleel.com is a few static pages. It sets no cookies and has no analytics, trackers, advertising, external fonts or third-party scripts. It is hosted on Cloudflare, which, like any web host, handles your IP address and the page you asked for in order to deliver it and keep the service secure. If you email the developer, he will have your address and what you wrote, and uses them only to answer you.

13Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the new version is posted here with a new date, and the history of this page is public in Mach's source repository. If Mach ever changes how it uses Google user data, you will be told in the app and asked to agree before the change applies to you. The same goes for Microsoft data.
